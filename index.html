AI Work Quality Platform

(Team Review & Standards SaaS)

AI made the work fluent. Someone still has to make it good.

When anyone can produce a polished page in thirty seconds, managers end up rewriting everything. This is the quality system for teams that use AI: written standards, visible thinking, fair review, and a manager who is not drowning.

The problem it solves

Poor work used to show its thinking, so a manager could correct it. AI-written filler reads well and says nothing, so it has to be redone.

Without a system

Someone pastes the task into a chatbot and forwards the result. The manager cannot find an argument to fix, so they rewrite it, for every person, every week, while being told AI made the team more productive.

With this platform

Every piece arrives with the author's own point and what they checked. It is judged against a written standard. The cost of each rewrite is recorded, and the manager can pause AI for the people it is not helping.

See it in four minutes

A middle manager reviews two submissions: one that has to be redone and one that is excellent. He pauses AI for one team member, then looks at standards, exemplars, the skill matrix, institutional memory and the weekly pulse. Then the same system from a junior analyst's seat, and from a leader's. The team and its data are fictional.

How it works

Judge the work, not the tool.

1

Write down the standard

Leaders define what good looks like for each kind of work, as weighted criteria.

2

Show the thinking

Authors state their one point and what they checked. A critic asks questions; they answer.

3

Review fairly

Managers score against the standard and say: approve, edit, or redo. Their time is logged.

4

Act on the pattern

Set each person's AI mode, coach, mentor, and watch the rewrite rate and the load fall.

Everything in one place

Connected tools for leaders, managers and the people doing the work.

Thinking trail and critic

  • No submission without thinking: the author's one point and what they personally checked.
  • A critic that only asks. It never writes the work.
  • Accept or push back in writing; the reviewer reads the exchange.
  • Slop-risk signals: stock phrases, no specifics, no reasoning.

Review: draft or rewrite?

  • Review queue with the thinking trail shown first.
  • Scored against the standard, criterion by criterion.
  • Three verdicts: approve, return to edit, needs a rewrite.
  • Minutes logged for reviewing and for redoing.

AI mode per person

  • Full, copilot only, or paused, set by the person's manager.
  • A reason is required, and the person sees it.
  • Enforced at submission, not left to memory.
  • Suggestions to pause, restore or coach, from review history.

Standards and exemplars

  • Rubric library for memos, analysis, slides and messages.
  • Exemplars: who did excellent work, and exactly how.
  • "I want to know how": curiosity instead of mandates.

Mastery and mentoring

  • Skill matrix: if you cannot do it, you cannot check it.
  • Core skills must be learned before AI may draft them.
  • Growth plans with a mentor, milestones and AI guidance.

For leaders

  • Manager load: hours reviewing and redoing, by week and by manager.
  • Better, not faster: quality by how AI was used.
  • Institutional memory: who to call, why it was decided, who objects.
  • Anonymous weekly pulse with an engagement score.

Good to know: the slop-risk score is a transparent text heuristic. It measures how writing reads, not whether it is correct, and it is not an AI detector; the reviewer always decides. The critic runs on your own server with no external AI service, and can optionally use a local model. Multi-tenant with leader, manager and member roles. Billing is not connected in this version.
