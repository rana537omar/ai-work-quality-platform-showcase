## The problem

Poor work used to show its thinking, so a manager could correct it and send it back. AI-written filler reads well and says nothing: there is no line of reasoning to fix, so the manager redoes it. Multiply that by every person on a team, every week.

The platform does not try to detect or ban AI. It asks a different question: **is the work good, and can its author stand behind it?**

## How it works

1. **Write down the standard.** Leaders define what good looks like for each kind of work, as weighted criteria.
2. **Show the thinking.** Authors state their one point and what they personally checked. A critic asks questions and they answer.
3. **Review fairly.** Managers score against the standard and choose: approve, return to edit, or needs a rewrite. Their time is logged.
4. **Act on the pattern.** Set each person's AI mode, coach, mentor, and watch the rewrite rate and the review load.

## Features

### Thinking trail and critic
- **No submission without thinking**: the author's one point, what they checked themselves, and what they are unsure about.
- **A critic that only asks questions.** It never writes the work.
- **Accept or push back** on each question in writing; the reviewer reads the exchange.
- **Slop-risk signals**: stock phrases, missing specifics, no visible reasoning, bullets without substance, and whether the text matches the stated point.

### Review: draft or rewrite?
- **Review queue** that shows the thinking trail before the work.
- **Scoring against the written standard**, criterion by criterion.
- **Three verdicts**: approve, return to edit, needs a rewrite.
- **Minutes logged** for reviewing and for redoing the work yourself.

### AI mode per person
- **Full AI, copilot only, or AI paused**, set by the person's own manager.
- **A reason is required**, and the person sees who changed it and why.
- **Enforced at submission**: locked options in the editor and a gate on submit.
- **Suggestions** to pause, restore or coach, based on recent reviews. The manager decides.

### Standards and exemplars
- **Rubric library** with starter standards for memos, analysis, presentations and external messages.
- **Exemplars**: who did excellent work, the result, and exactly how they did it, including how AI was or was not used.
- **"I want to know how"**: curiosity instead of mandates.

### Mastery and mentoring
- **Skill matrix** from "not yet" to "master", set by managers.
- **The outsourcing rule**: on a core skill someone is still learning, AI may not write the draft, because they could not check the result.
- **Growth plans** with a mentor, milestones and per-person guidance on using AI while learning.

### For leaders
- **Manager load**: hours spent reviewing and redoing, by week and by manager, with alerts.
- **Better, not faster**: quality compared by how AI was used.
- **Institutional memory**: who to call, why a rule exists, who will object and why.
- **Anonymous weekly pulse** with an engagement score. Weeks with fewer than three answers are hidden.

### Built as a SaaS
- **Multi-tenant**: each organisation has its own workspace, people and data.
- **Three roles**: leader, manager and team member, each with their own view and permissions.
- **Private**: the critic runs on the server with no external AI service, and can optionally use a local model.

### Good to know
- The slop-risk score is a transparent text heuristic. It measures how writing reads, not whether it is correct, and it is not an AI detector. The reviewer always decides.
- Billing is not connected in this version.
